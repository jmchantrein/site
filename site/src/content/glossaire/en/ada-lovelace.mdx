Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace (1815-1852), daughter of the poet Byron, was trained in mathematics by Mary Somerville and Augustus De Morgan — an exceptional path for a woman in Victorian England.

In 1843 she translated Luigi Menabrea's paper on Charles Babbage's **Analytical Engine** and expanded it with seven notes, longer than the paper itself. Note G contains a complete algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers on the machine: the first published program — for a computer that would never be built.

Her most visionary contribution is not the program but the idea that the machine could handle **arbitrary symbols**, not just numbers: music, text… She described general-purpose computing a century in advance. She also stated a famous limit (the machine "has no pretensions whatever to originate anything" — it executes what we know how to prescribe), which Turing discussed in 1950 as "Lady Lovelace's objection".

The **Ada** language (1980), commissioned by the US Department of Defense, bears her name.
