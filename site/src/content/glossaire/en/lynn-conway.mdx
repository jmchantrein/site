Lynn Conway (1938-2024) had two careers in one. At IBM in the 1960s she invented **dynamic instruction scheduling**, a key to superscalar processors — then was fired in 1968 when she announced her gender transition. She started over, at the bottom of the ladder, under a new identity.

At Xerox PARC, with Carver Mead, she co-wrote *Introduction to VLSI Systems* (1980) and invented the methodology that let university teams design complex chips — including the MPC/MOSIS multi-project fabrication service. This "**VLSI** revolution" made modern microprocessors possible and trained an entire generation of designers.

Her role at IBM was only publicly acknowledged in the early 2000s, when she chose to tell her story; IBM formally apologized in 2020 — 52 years later. She devoted the end of her life to defending the rights of trans people in the sciences.
