Grace Murray Hopper (1906-1992), a Yale PhD in mathematics, joined the US Navy in 1943 and programmed the Harvard Mark I. It is in her team's logbook that the moth of the "first literal bug" was taped in 1947.

Her decisive contribution lies elsewhere: convinced that programming should happen in an English-like language, she wrote one of the first **compilers** (A-0) in 1952, then led FLOW-MATIC — the direct ancestor of **COBOL** (1959), whose standardization she steered. The idea of a program translating a readable language into machine instructions, deemed unrealistic by her contemporaries, is now the bedrock of all computing.

A rear admiral, she remains famous for her "nanoseconds": 30 cm pieces of wire she handed out to make people feel the distance light travels in a billionth of a second — a memory-hierarchy lesson ahead of its time.
